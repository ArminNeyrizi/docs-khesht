# Governance and authority

## Purpose

This document defines the governance rules connecting board oversight, executive management, and operational delivery. Use it with the approved organization chart, job descriptions, and Delegation of Authority (DoA) matrix.

## Accountability model

| Role | Accountable for | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Board / owners | Strategy, reserved decisions, oversight, executive appointment | Routine execution unless explicitly delegated |
| CEO | Strategy execution, resources, cross-unit performance, IMS effectiveness | Matters reserved to the board |
| Business / function lead | Results, capacity, risks, and controls within approved scope | Unapproved spending, hiring, contracts, or scope changes |
| Process owner | Process outcomes, controls, documentation, and improvement | Overriding legal, safety, contract, or financial controls |
| Project / service manager | Delivery against approved scope, time, cost, quality, and customer requirements | Unapproved commitments or technical changes |
| Control function | Defined review, advice, verification, and specialist controls | Automatic ownership of line-management results |

The approved DoA matrix takes precedence over assumptions based on job titles. If authority is undocumented, escalate to an authorized decision-maker rather than infer approval.

## Decision record

Material decisions record the ID and date; requester and decision-maker; question and options; financial, customer, schedule, quality, safety, legal, and capacity implications as applicable; evidence and assumptions; decision (approve, reject, revise, hold, or stop); conditions; action owners; due dates; and links to affected projects, processes, risks, contracts, or changes.

## Delegation of Authority

The DoA matrix must define thresholds and approval rights for budget and budget changes; purchases, payments, and write-offs; proposals, discounts, and contracts; hiring and compensation; project scope and schedule changes; technical deviations and quality release; safety-critical work and stop-work decisions; sensitive information access; production-system changes; and new initiatives or investments.

Each row states the decision, proposer, reviewer, approver, executor, threshold, evidence required, and escalation path. Monetary thresholds must be approved by leadership, not invented in this document.

## Escalation

Escalate immediately when a decision exceeds delegated authority, threatens life or safety, may breach law or contract, materially affects cash commitments, compromises data integrity, or makes an approved delivery commitment unachievable.

## Management review

The CEO should convene a documented review at a defined cadence. The first review establishes the cadence rather than assuming one already exists.

Inputs include strategic objectives and KPIs; customer feedback; delivery, quality, schedule, and cost; major risks; nonconformities and corrective actions; audit results and obligations; resource and competence gaps; and proposed changes.

Outputs include decisions, resource commitments, action owners, due dates, and follow-up evidence. A meeting without recorded decisions and action tracking is not a complete management-review record.
