# Engineering service delivery

## Purpose

Define minimum control gates for engineering services and project work. Project-specific plans, approved technical methods, statutory requirements, and client contracts may impose additional controls.

## Delivery workflow

| Gate | Required activity | Minimum evidence | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 — Qualification | Understand client, need, fit, and opportunity | Lead record and qualification outcome | Pursue, decline, or request information |
| G2 — Feasibility | Assess technical approach, capacity, schedule, risk, and commercial viability | Feasibility assessment and assumptions | Proceed, revise, hold, or stop |
| G3 — Proposal approval | Define scope, exclusions, deliverables, acceptance criteria, price, and terms | Reviewed proposal and approval | Authorize issue or revise |
| G4 — Contract review | Confirm obligations, interfaces, changes, payment terms, and authority | Contract review checklist and approved agreement | Accept, negotiate, or reject |
| G5 — Mobilization | Assign lead, team, budget, plan, and quality requirements | Project plan, role assignments, baseline | Authorize start |
| G6 — Execution and control | Manage work, interfaces, progress, risks, reviews, and changes | Work products, progress records, review evidence, change log | Continue, correct, or escalate |
| G7 — Verification | Check deliverables against approved requirements | Inspection/review records and defect disposition | Accept, rework, or escalate |
| G8 — Handover and closeout | Transfer deliverables, confirm acceptance, close issues, reconcile commercial status, capture lessons | Handover record, open-item list, closure report | Close or retain controlled actions |

## Scope and change control

Do not implement a material change to scope, price, schedule, design basis, deliverables, or acceptance criteria without an impact assessment and approval under the DoA and contract.

The change record identifies the request, reason, affected baseline, technical and commercial impact, risks, decision-maker, approval, implementation owner, and updated documents. Emergency changes require an approved expedited-approval and retrospective-documentation rule; they must not bypass safety or legal requirements.

## Technical and quality controls

At project start, define which deliverables require independent checking, interdisciplinary coordination, client review, site verification, testing, or formal acceptance. The control plan specifies the competent role and evidence to retain.

Do not represent a deliverable as approved while required checks remain incomplete. Record, assess, disposition, and verify deviations and defects before release where required.

## Schedule, cost, and capacity

Establish a baseline appropriate to project size. Track progress and forecast completion against it, record material variance, and assign corrective actions. Resource allocation must account for competence and availability, not only nominal headcount. Do not hide delay by changing a baseline without an approved change.

## Customer communication and acceptance

Record material customer requirements, clarifications, approvals, complaints, and acceptance. Define who can make commitments on behalf of Khesht. Confirm verbal instructions affecting scope, cost, schedule, or technical requirements through the agreed communication channel.

## Closeout

Confirm required deliverables and records are handed over, defects and residual risks are dispositioned, customer acceptance is documented where applicable, financial follow-up is assigned, and lessons learned are recorded.

## Required project records

Maintain the opportunity and qualification record, feasibility assessment, approved proposal, contract review, scope and baseline, responsibility assignments, risk register, technical review and quality records, change log, progress reports, customer communications, handover evidence, and closeout record. Apply access and retention rules appropriate to contractual and legal requirements.
